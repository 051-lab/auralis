# Auralis Responsible Audio Guidelines

Auralis is an experimental browser-based sound and wellness tool. It is not medical software.

These guidelines should shape presets, UI copy, documentation, and future audio features.

## Core Safety Principles

- Auralis is not a diagnostic, therapeutic, or clinical device.
- Start at low volume, especially with headphones.
- Stop listening if discomfort, dizziness, headache, anxiety, ear pain, or irritation occurs.
- Use headphones carefully and take breaks.
- Avoid long sessions at high volume.
- Do not use Auralis while driving, operating machinery, or doing tasks that require full attention.
- Users with neurological, hearing, vestibular, or medical concerns should use extra caution and consult qualified professionals when needed.

## Claims to Avoid

Do not claim that Auralis or any preset:

- Will cause a brain state.
- Will heal a condition.
- Is guaranteed to work.
- Treats anxiety, depression, insomnia, ADHD, trauma, tinnitus, or any medical condition.
- Diagnoses or monitors health.
- Replaces professional care.
- Puts the brain into theta, alpha, gamma, or any other state.

Avoid wording like:

- "will cause"
- "will heal"
- "guaranteed"
- "treats anxiety"
- "cures insomnia"
- "puts your brain into theta"
- "medically proven"
- "therapy"

## Preferred Language

Use careful language that describes design intent rather than guaranteed outcomes.

Preferred wording:

- "designed around"
- "inspired by"
- "intended for"
- "may support"
- "may help some listeners"
- "headphone-focused"
- "for quiet listening"
- "for exploration"
- "experimental"

Examples:

```text
Designed around a 10 Hz interaural frequency difference.
```

```text
Alpha-inspired preset intended for quiet focus sessions.
```

```text
May support a calm listening environment for some users.
```

## Binaural Beat Language

Binaural beats and related auditory beat phenomena have mixed research support and variable listener response.

Use:

- "binaural-style"
- "interaural frequency difference"
- "headphones recommended"
- "designed around a 6 Hz difference"
- "inspired by theta-band terminology"

Avoid:

- "entrains your brain"
- "forces theta"
- "guaranteed gamma state"
- "clinically treats"

## Photosensitive and Neurological Caution

Auralis is primarily audio-focused, but the app includes animated visuals.

Guidelines:

- Avoid intense flashing.
- Avoid claims about neurological treatment.
- Keep visualizer motion smooth rather than strobing.
- Consider a future reduced-motion mode.
- Use caution with language around seizures, migraine, and neurological conditions.

## Headphone Guidance

Binaural-style presets require independent left/right headphone presentation to work as designed.

Recommended copy:

```text
Use stereo headphones for binaural-style presets. Start at low volume and stop if the sound feels uncomfortable.
```

Avoid:

```text
Headphones will put your brain into the target state.
```

## Preset Review Checklist

Before adding a preset, check:

- Does the name avoid medical claims?
- Does the description say "inspired by" or "designed around" instead of "causes"?
- Are gain levels conservative?
- Is headphone guidance included for binaural-style presets?
- Are reverb and panning choices compatible with the preset goal?
- Is noise level low enough to avoid discomfort?
- Does the preset avoid guaranteeing results?

## Product Positioning

Auralis should be described as:

- A browser-based sound laboratory.
- A somatic frequency and listening tool.
- An experimental audio environment.
- A preset-based tone, noise, modulation, and spatial listening system.

Auralis should not be described as:

- Medical treatment.
- Brain therapy.
- A clinical entrainment device.
- A cure.
- A diagnostic system.
